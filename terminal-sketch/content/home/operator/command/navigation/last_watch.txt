MIRA // LAST WATCH

I left my flight notebook beside the console. There is still fuel. Every time I sketch the return, I picture the lights along the coast. Then I remember the sealed berths behind us. We never established who was sleeping in them.

The captain calls the present heading containment. I watched the Sun grow on the forward display and tried to give that word a human face. I could not. I cannot tell the families that we were certain.

He still uses the same pause before saying my name. I heard it through the wall after they carried his body away. Someone in Command kept a record of that night. I have not been able to read it.

The isolation panel is still wired to his continuity links. The course computer has its own supply. I checked that twice, then closed the cover. I wanted silence. I did not know whether I wanted his death.

If Samuel wakes, I hope he reads what we actually wrote. We have left him enough confident voices already.
