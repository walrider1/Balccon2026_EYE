UNSENT // FOR SAMUEL

You used to call yourself the least interesting Slovak in Vojvodina. Then you would spend an hour explaining how a root survives where nothing should. I fell in love with the man who remembered to warm my hands before telling me I had watered his experiment to death.

I thought your silences were peace. Once I came back early and saw what you were extracting, what you swallowed. You hid it before you saw my face. Later I understood how much of that effortless calm you were making for yourself. I should have asked you for the truth instead of protecting our comfortable version of you.

After Mars, you did the same thing at a crowded workbench. Same hands, same little joke. No attempt to hide. You remembered what you did, but seemed to have forgotten why it had been a secret between us. That frightened me. It was not proof. I keep writing that because everyone else has stopped saying it.

I do not know whether the man begging us to believe him was you. I know that I helped choose the man who sounded familiar. If you ever read this, please do not let familiarity make that choice for you again.
